Interview with: Priya Nair, Senior Loan Officer, Retail Lending
Topic: New digital personal-loan origination system

Priya: The main goal here is to let a customer apply for a personal loan
entirely online and get a decision the same day, instead of the current
process where a branch officer manually checks everything and it takes
three to five business days.

Before we even show the application form, the customer has to complete
KYC. If their government ID and address proof don't match our records, or
if they've failed the automated ID check twice already, we cannot let them
submit a loan application — that has to go to manual review by someone on
the compliance team.

Once KYC is done, the customer fills in the loan amount, tenure, and
purpose. We run a credit check and an affordability calculation
automatically. If the requested amount is under our auto-approval limit
and the applicant's risk score is good, the system should approve it
without a human ever looking at it. Above that limit, or if the fraud
model flags anything unusual, a credit officer has to review it manually
and can override the system's recommendation — but they have to write down
why, we can't just let someone silently overrule the model.

If we reject an application, the customer needs to actually be told why in
writing before we finalize things — we can't just say "declined." That's
partly a regulatory thing, partly just fair treatment.

On the money side: once approved, the loan amount has to go directly into
the bank account that's registered in the applicant's own name — we've had
issues in the past with disbursing into someone else's account by
accident, so I want that hard blocked, no exceptions, not even for
"family member" requests.

For security, my counterpart in InfoSec told me that any change to a
customer's registered bank account or phone number should require the
customer to re-verify with a second factor, and both the old and new
contact method should get notified. Also, anyone who is a politically
exposed person needs extra sign-off from compliance before we can even
onboard them — that can't be automatic.

We also need an audit trail. For every application, I want to be able to
go back later and see exactly what data was used, what the credit model
decided, and who — human or system — approved or rejected it, with a
timestamp. Our regulator has asked us for this more than once during
inspections.

On performance: the credit decision itself should come back to the
customer in under five seconds for the auto-approved path. Manual review
cases obviously take longer, that's fine, but customers should at least
see their application status update within a minute of a reviewer taking
an action.

Data-wise, we keep completed loan records for at least seven years for
audit purposes, even if the customer later asks us to delete their data —
we just have to be upfront that the loan record itself is retained
separately.

Lastly, we want this to plug into our existing core banking system for
disbursement and our existing CRM for customer records — we don't want a
totally separate database of customers.
